Market Overview
Q3 Earnings Kick Off Strong — But the Bond Market Is the Real Story
Equities posted a solid week as big bank earnings impressed, while a sharp Treasury selloff sent the 10-year to 4.10% — its highest level since early August.

Q3 2026 earnings season opened with a bang. JPMorgan reported EPS of $4.37 vs. $4.01 expected, driven by strong investment banking fees (+31% YoY) and resilient net interest income. Wells Fargo beat on NII guidance; BlackRock's AUM climbed to a record $11.5T on equity market gains and net inflows. The early read: corporate America remains in solid shape, but management teams are universally flagging elevated macro uncertainty heading into year-end.

The bigger market story, however, was Treasuries. The 10-year yield spiked 14 basis points on the week to 4.10% — partly on strong CPI data (headline +2.4% YoY, above the 2.3% estimate) and partly on rising term premium as investors demanded more compensation for holding long-duration debt. The move rattled rate-sensitive sectors but paradoxically supported financials, which benefit from a steeper yield curve. Fed Chair Powell reiterated a data-dependent stance; markets are now pricing just one additional 25bp cut before year-end.

Asset Management
BlackRock Hits $11.5T AUM — A Record Built on Private Markets and ETFs
Q3 results confirm the firm's diversification strategy is working even as markets grow uncertain about rates and the election.

BlackRock reported Q3 2026 results this week with total AUM hitting a record $11.5 trillion — up from $10.6T a year ago. Net inflows of $221B were the highest in two years, driven by ETF demand ($102B) and continued institutional allocation to private markets infrastructure funds. The GIP acquisition has paid off: infrastructure AUM now sits at $170B, and the firm is pitching a $30B infrastructure equity fund targeting sovereign wealth funds and pension plans.

For AWMA members, the broader lesson is in portfolio construction: institutions are increasingly treating infrastructure and private credit as a "fourth asset class" alongside equities, fixed income, and cash. With traditional 60/40 portfolios facing headwinds from both high rates and elevated equity valuations, allocators are using real assets as a diversifier. In interviews, be prepared to discuss why private markets have grown so rapidly and what liquidity trade-offs investors accept in exchange for the illiquidity premium.

Wealth Management
Rising Rates Force Advisors to Revisit Client Bond Allocations in Real Time
The 10-year yield's sharp move to 4.10% has real implications for client portfolios — and is generating some of the most instructive WM conversations of the year.

The Treasury selloff this week caught many wealth management clients off-guard. Investors who shifted into longer-duration bonds earlier in 2026 — expecting rate cuts — are now sitting on mark-to-market losses as the 10-year rises. This has triggered a wave of client calls at major wealth platforms, with advisors at Morgan Stanley, Merrill, and Raymond James all reporting elevated inbound volume around bond allocation questions.

The practical WM lesson: duration risk is portfolio risk, and client communication around it matters as much as the underlying positioning decision. Wells Fargo Wealth Management published a client note this week recommending a barbell approach — holding shorter-duration Treasuries alongside equities rather than extending duration in anticipation of cuts. For AWMA members, understanding how to explain bond math (price moves inversely with yield, duration measures sensitivity) in plain language is a core advisor competency. Expect this topic to come up in interviews — "walk me through how you'd explain the bond sell-off to a client" is a live question.

Industry Briefs
Asset Management
Q3 Earnings Signal Private Markets Are Now Core, Not Alternative
BlackRock's record $11.5T AUM and $221B in net inflows confirm that institutional allocators have decisively shifted toward private markets. Infrastructure, private credit, and real assets are no longer treated as alternatives — they are structural portfolio positions. Ares Management and Apollo also reported strong quarters, with each firm highlighting robust demand for investment-grade private credit from insurance companies and pension funds. The trend has implications for students: AM roles increasingly require comfort with illiquid structures, NAV calculations, and the mechanics of drawdown funds — concepts that were once the domain of specialized alternatives teams but are now mainstream in multi-asset portfolios.
Wealth Management
Morgan Stanley Wealth Reports $6.2T in Client Assets — Advisor Productivity at Record
Morgan Stanley's Q3 earnings highlighted the continued strength of its Wealth Management segment, which reported $6.2T in client assets and net new assets of $64B. Advisor productivity — revenue per advisor — hit an all-time high as the firm's technology platform investments paid off in client acquisition and retention. CEO Ted Pick emphasized that Morgan Stanley's differentiated model — integrating wealth management with investment banking and asset management — is a structural competitive advantage that peers cannot easily replicate. For WM-track members, this is useful context for firm-specific interview prep: MS's pitch is that it's not just an advisory firm, but an integrated financial services company that gives advisors proprietary deal flow and research that independent or bank-affiliated competitors cannot match.
Private Markets
Apollo Targets $1.5T AUM by 2029 — Retirement Capital Is the Engine
Apollo Global Management reiterated its $1.5T AUM target by 2029 (from ~$733B today) at its investor day this week, outlining a strategy centered on retirement capital — specifically, using its Athene insurance subsidiary to source $100B+ in annual origination capacity for investment-grade private credit. The firm argued that the traditional bank lending model is structurally retreating due to Basel III capital requirements, leaving a $40T+ gap that alternative asset managers are positioned to fill. For students targeting private credit or alternatives roles, Apollo's model is worth studying closely: it shows how insurance float, origination platforms, and credit expertise combine into a scalable alternatives business that is increasingly challenging traditional banking.
Macro & Policy
CPI Comes in Hot — One More Reason the Fed Is in No Rush
September CPI surprised to the upside at +2.4% year-over-year (vs. +2.3% expected), with core CPI holding at +3.3%. The data triggered a sharp selloff in Treasuries — the 10-year rose 14 bps on the week — and caused futures markets to reprice the expected pace of Fed rate cuts. Markets are now pricing just one additional 25bp cut before year-end, versus two cuts that were priced a month ago. The "higher for longer" dynamic that defined 2025 is showing signs of re-emerging. For members, the key interview concept is the difference between what the Fed wants (inflation at 2%) and what the market prices (the path to get there). Right now, those two things are diverging — and that divergence is driving most of the volatility in rates and rate-sensitive equities.
Global Macro & Geopolitics
🇺🇸 U.S. · Election & Policy
Markets Are Beginning to Price Election Outcomes — Financials and Energy Lead
With the election three weeks away, markets are pricing scenario outcomes by sector. Financials have outperformed on expectations of lighter regulatory oversight; health care has lagged on drug pricing uncertainty; energy has quietly added a political risk premium. For students, election-driven sector rotation is a live portfolio construction challenge: how do you position for political uncertainty without making a directional bet? The answer most PMs give — own quality, reduce duration, stay diversified — is an important interview talking point on risk management when macro is uncertain.

🇨🇳 China · Stimulus & Growth
China's Stimulus Package Disappoints — Emerging Market Allocators Reassess
China's NDRC unveiled its long-awaited fiscal stimulus package this week — and markets sold off hard on the lack of new specifics. The package offered broad language about infrastructure and consumption support but no new spending figures beyond what was announced in September. Chinese equities fell 7% on the week, erasing much of the September rally. For AM students, this is instructive: stimulus announcements move markets on expectations, not results. The difference between policy announcement risk and implementation risk is a nuanced concept that separates strong EM analysts from weaker ones in interviews.
⚠ Elevated Risk
🇪🇺 Europe · ECB & Credit
ECB Cuts Again — But European Credit Spreads Widen on Growth Concerns
The ECB cut its deposit rate 25bp to 3.25% — its third cut of 2026 — citing softening eurozone growth. Germany's manufacturing PMI held in contraction at 40.6; France reported weak industrial output. Despite the cut, European high-yield credit spreads widened ~30bps on growth fears, a live example of how policy and credit can diverge: cuts should tighten spreads, but when recession risk dominates, credit sells off regardless. The ECB-Fed divergence is also driving FX volatility — EUR/USD fell toward 1.09 — adding complexity for global fixed income PMs.
